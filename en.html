From debut to comeback

Train your trainees and form a group. Pick a concept and a producer, decide the music video budget, and the comeback begins.

Money alone never buys a #1. Every producer has concepts they are good at, and when you fall short of expectations the headline reads "all that money for that rank?".

The moment your group takes the stage

The members you picked dance to the song you produced. Change the concept and the set and choreography change too.

On the night the chart drops, shake your iPhone like a light stick. The harder you shake, the louder the crowd. When the point choreography goes viral, the views and comments pile up in front of you.

Will this week go quietly?

The week you are nominated for a music show win, a rival announces a comeback on the same day. A member falls during a live broadcast.

Each time you choose how to respond, and a short duel of dice or timing decides the outcome.

The first two years are survival. Miss the promise to your investor and you hand over part of the company.

Year seven, when your choices come back

Every member has a story. One supports a family back home, another was cut from a different agency.

What you hear in one-on-one talks and what you decide in a crisis builds loyalty, and that comes back at the contract renewal table in year seven.

A member who only does variety shows may leave as a TV personality, and a neglected foreign member may go home.

Ten years on, your agency

As the fandom grows the stage widens, from small theaters to a dome and from Tokyo to New York. At the year-end awards you open the envelope yourself.

You might be remembered as a legend, or end as a one-hit wonder or a closing press conference.

Twenty endings stay in your collection and become the legacy of your next run.
